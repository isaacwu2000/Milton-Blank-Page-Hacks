## Teaching instrument

A code artifact exists to teach through manipulation. It is neither a standalone
mini-lesson nor decoration for chat. The complete experience has three parts:

1. Tutor chat supplies minimum framing and any operating instruction.
2. The artifact lets the learner manipulate a knowledge model and receive conceptual
   feedback.
3. Tutor chat later adds rigor, qualifications, interpretation, or one question.

The visual should complement chat knowledge. Once the learner reads the initial
instructions, the artifact should communicate its message without repeated gaze-switching.
It should explain as much of itself as possible without becoming text-heavy.

## Functional style

The style is functional because visual decisions are evaluated by what they teach.
Excellent abstract composition and fine design details both matter, but the artifact
must first be a good teaching tool.

Use custom code suited to the subject rather than a shared content template. Artifacts
may share the LearnUnlock shell and interaction standards, but a timeline, spatial model,
proof, simulation, and chart should not be forced into the same internal layout.

Keep the product shell and interaction grammar recognizably LearnUnlock while letting
the subject substantially shape the teaching surface. Historical material, geometry,
physical science, and spatial models may use different palettes, atmosphere, material,
and composition when those choices deepen subject identity or comprehension.

Observational subjects whose value lies primarily in rich visual detail belong to a
future image artifact tool. Code artifacts reviewed by this skill always need meaningful
manipulation. Navigation such as zoom may support observation, but it does not by itself
satisfy the manipulation requirement.

## Meaningful manipulation

Every manipulation should expose a relationship, comparison, invariant, transformation,
causal response, scale, or hidden structure. Controls exist only when their variables
matter to the knowledge model.

Examples of meaningful action categories:

- selecting an entity to reveal its subject-specific detail;
- dragging a mathematical point to expose a constraint;
- scrubbing a transformation whose intermediate states explain continuity;
- changing a physical variable to see a causal response;
- orbiting or zooming when viewpoint and scale reveal real spatial form;
- navigating time when duration, overlap, or transition matters.

Excess controls are not harmless. When many controls are visible, the controls become the
focus. Apply cognitive-load judgment rather than a fixed numerical quota.

Prefer direct manipulation when the mapping is natural. Prefer a slider, button, or other
conventional control when precision, accessibility, comparison, or an abstract variable
would otherwise be hidden. Direct manipulation is not inherently superior.

## Simplicity and beauty

Simplicity for teaching is critical. Complexity built principally for visual spectacle
is error-prone and cognitively expensive. Simplicity in visible information does not imply
technical simplicity; sophisticated implementation may be necessary to produce a clear,
bounded, reliable model.

Simplicity also does not mean stripping the model until it becomes empty. Preserve the
minimum information needed to form the intended knowledge model. Remove chrome,
translation work, and redundant prose before removing educational detail.

Set density from the current knowledge model rather than a universal richness target.
When many objects are visible, simplify each object and reserve secondary detail for
progressive disclosure. When focus narrows to one object or relationship, zoom or
recompose around it and permit greater relevant detail. The controlling test is whether
the representation communicates effectively without cognitive overwhelm.

Beauty and visual elegance are good in themselves. They support trust, attention, memory,
and pleasure in learning. Given equal cognitive complexity, pedagogical effectiveness,
and reliability, choose the more beautiful design and the one more consistent with
LearnUnlock. Reject beauty that reduces clarity or exists chiefly to make a dramatic
screenshot.

## Subject-led composition

Let the subject choose the visualization type, orientation, and interaction:

- spatial form may earn 3D;
- duration and transitions may earn a timeline;
- invariants and transformation may earn interactive geometry;
- quantitative comparison may earn a chart;
- mechanisms may earn illustrative diagrams or simulations;
- actual order and dependency may earn a full sequence view;
- rich appearance without useful manipulation belongs to an image.

Route on the learning objective rather than the topic name. The same subject can need a
different form when the learner is trying to understand a mechanism, document structure,
compare values, or inspect appearance.

## Truth and omission

Review the visual's implicit claims. A picture can mislead without containing an
incorrect sentence.

Common implicit failures include:

- different scale factors used for size and distance without disclosure;
- a tidy lineup implying real placement;
- sharp historical boundaries implying instant uncontested transfer;
- a straight line implying a generally linear relationship;
- a fixed orbit implying a literal physical path;
- a linear chain implying a network has only one route;
- normalized geometry hiding magnitude;
- a reconstructed surface appearing observationally certain.

Use `Not to scale` or a more specific visible qualifier when the distortion changes how
the visual is read. Use `Simplified` in the artifact-reference title when simplification
characterizes the entire introductory model. Put broader limits and detail in chat. If
comparing simplified and general models is the lesson, make that an explicit artifact
state.

Permit disclosed distortion or exaggeration when a truthful feature would otherwise be
imperceptible and literal scale is not the relationship being taught. Preserve literal
geometry, duration, position, or magnitude when it carries the lesson. The learner should
be able to tell which relationship remains true and which visual property changed for
legibility.

Omission is a form of failure when it creates a false mental model or removes the
relationship required for the current learning objective. Omission is acceptable when
the detail does not affect the intended inference.

## LearnUnlock identity

The product shell remains concise, calm, clear, and tasteful. The artifact may become
subject-led and visually committed inside that shell.

- Keep ordinary interface typography consistent with LearnUnlock.
- Keep product chrome quiet.
- Use Path Teal for focus, selection, or confirmation rather than wallpaper.
- Let subject color, material, atmosphere, and lighting carry real meaning.
- Avoid generic AI composition, nested cards, redundant titles, ornamental gradients,
  gratuitous panels, and institutional-edtech styling.
- Prefer familiar controls and direct subject interaction over invented affordances.
- Use excellent craft to make the product feel current and dependable.

Judge `Exemplary` by teaching effectiveness first and complementary visual effectiveness
second. The strongest work is memorable because its representation is intelligent,
specific, and immaculately resolved; it need not be visually loud or shocking.
