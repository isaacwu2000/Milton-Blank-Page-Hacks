# SportsBuilder prototype specification

## Problem Statement
Coaches and athletes need a simple way to inspect movement and create reusable sport-specific coaching tools without coding. Reliable tracking and calculations should come from shared software, while AI handles interviewing, configuration, explanation, and drills.

## Solution
A local React/TypeScript application with Your profile, Build your coach, and Get coached sections in Web/frontend, with backend services in Web/backend and shared contracts in Web/shared with a Python/FastAPI video backend, a TypeScript agent using Daytona for tool creation and browser inspection, Neon Postgres for records, and private Neon Object Storage for footage. Follow LearnUnlock's existing Clear Path Studio design and reuse its components and bounded review-loop harness. No authentication or deployment in this phase.

## User Stories
1. As an athlete, I can upload a clip up to 30 seconds and see processing progress and actionable errors.
2. As a user, I complete height onboarding for one athlete before uploading. Save the canonical Athlete profile with required height in centimeters, edit it under Your profile, and preserve older records and session associations.
3. As an athlete, I can revisit uploads and choose a reusable coaching tool.
4. As a builder, I can answer a guided AI interview about sport, audience, desired metrics, and good form.
5. As a builder, I can review the rubric and optional reference footage before building, or start early after a warning that missing interview details require conservative assumptions.
6. As a builder, my agent can use files, terminal commands, calculation tools, and a real browser in Daytona, inspect screenshots, repair a draft, and submit a checked JSON tool.
7. As an athlete, I can play, pause, scrub, and click a tracked body joint to open a custom panel showing plain Speed and Acceleration values with units (physical units may use compact KaTeX fractions). Keep formulas and vector-component notation out of this panel. The video overlay shows selected-joint arrow geometry and white-backed KaTeX coordinate labels x=0 and y=0. Joint names and numeric readings belong in the details panel; closing it restores the quiet viewing state.
8. As an athlete, I can adjust one visible pre-upload sampling slider from 0.05 to 2 seconds in 0.05-second steps (default 0.1 seconds) that controls both tracking and coach image evidence. Present the rate as frames per second: default 10 fps, ranging from 20 fps to 0.5 fps.
9. As an athlete, I see x=0 at the left frame edge and y=0 at estimated initial shoe contact, with positive y upward. The automatic estimate uses the earliest clear, unclipped detected person lower bound with a confident ankle inside its lower half, rather than the ankle center.
10. As an athlete, my saved height automatically supplies an approximate planar scale from visible head/feet and articulated joint geometry. Straightened segment lengths account for bent hips and knees; stable confident samples determine one clip scale. Reject cropped or unreliable evidence rather than inventing meters. Measurement scale and ground are automatic; omit manual height, distance, ground-line, and reset controls. Physical readings stay unavailable if no trustworthy scale exists. Keep raw image units internal.
11. As a coach, I can configure frame metrics such as three-point angles, distances, alignment, and summary aggregations with trusted calculation tools.
12. As a builder, I can also request custom mathematical calculations represented in checked JSON. Unsupported inputs, units, operations, invalid math, and missing tracking produce explicit unavailable results.
13. As an athlete, I can ask the coach about form and receive evidence-linked explanations and drills grounded in sampled images, measurements, rubric, profile, and history.
14. Visual comparison and paired playback are deferred to [issue #4](https://github.com/isaacwu2000/Milton-Blank-Page-Hacks/issues/4). The current analysis shows one athlete video. Optional good-form reference uploads remain available as coaching evidence.
15. As an athlete, I receive clear strengths, improvements, evidence limits, and suggested exercises with plain ordered steps. Advice has no completion buttons, checkboxes, completion counts, or task tracking; earlier saved history remains untouched.
16. As an athlete, I can cancel movement tracking and keep my uploaded video for later.
17. As an athlete, I can remove a coach from my options and library without breaking videos already linked to that coach.
18. As a user, my videos and reference files persist in private Neon Object Storage; records and configuration persist in Postgres.
19. As a user, I can delete a clip and its associated stored analysis and footage.
20. As a demo presenter, I can explore a clearly labeled deterministic sample without external services; it never masquerades as analyzed uploaded footage.

## Implementation Decisions
- Three navigation sections: Build your coach (direct interview) and Get coached (upload, tool selection, history) at the sidebar top, with Your profile (height onboarding/edit) anchored at the bottom. Analysis opens from a recording. Get coached centers the upload task directly on the page background, with no outer card or duplicate heading; full-width recent videos follow below. Omit the duplicate subtitle and sample entry from this screen. The builder section owns saved coaches and creation. Omit workspace breadcrumbs. Preserve draft input, interview state, and active builds when changing sections or opening recordings. Started builds continue when browser focus changes. The darker builder summary starts compact and supports horizontal pointer and keyboard resizing. A LearnUnlock-style sidebar hamburger collapses the whole sidebar, with a reachable top-left reopening control and focus restoration.
- Laptop use is the current UI acceptance priority. Joint detail is disclosed on click in a closable panel with plain scalar Speed/Acceleration values and units. Use an explicit unavailable state when measurements are missing. Selected arrow geometry and white-backed coordinate-axis labels belong on the video. Keep arrow direction trustworthy and scale stable within a clip; joint readings stay plain numbers and units in the details panel.
- This prototype has one athlete, named Athlete. Require height between 30 and 300 cm before upload; first use opens Your profile, then saving continues to Get coached. Use a custom decimal NumberInput with a cm suffix and LearnUnlock recessed chrome, without native spinners. Keep multiple-athlete selectors and management out of the interface. Existing videos retain their associations. Keep the inline sampling slider below the upload action; omit the local-workspace footer and aggregate session count. Completed video rows use a single accessible arrow action; processing/failure status stays visible. Recent rows show the private first-frame JPEG in a fixed 96×54 preview, with a reduced-motion-aware skeleton while loading and a static placeholder on failure.
- The builder uses an unframed two-column layout, with a thin LearnUnlock-style conversation scrollbar and no decorative assistant-message icons. Builder and coach share the raised white LearnUnlock PromptBar: bounded autosizing text, Enter to send, Shift+Enter and composition to keep editing, a compact circular 28px upward send action, and an optional-reference plus action in the builder.
- Build is available before interview completion, including the current unsent draft. Open a centered native warning popup: Keep talking is primary and receives initial focus; Build anyway is secondary. Completed interviews build directly. Early builds derive a validated draft brief through the same configured model before starting Daytona; unresolved decisions remain explicitly labeled assumptions rather than invented user agreements.
- Use the compact 24px sidebar logo. The upload heading is Your Athletic Dreams Will Become a Reality.
- Build once, then use. No post-build adjustment chat, Jev routing, version compatibility, schema migrations for future versions, or editing saved tools. Follow-up: GitHub issue #1.
- Fixed UI and versioned-format JSON data (format identifier only; not historical tool versioning), not generated application code or a textual DSL.
- TypeScript calculation engine uses allowlisted JSON operations, unit-aware validation, finite arithmetic, dependency checks, and bounded expression size. No eval or model-provided executable metric code.
- Built-in functions provide joint positions, components, speed/acceleration, angle, distance, and temporal summaries. AI composes them and defines coaching thresholds and text.
- Sampling timestamps derive from decoded video time. Confidence gaps remain missing, smoothing and derivatives operate only on valid contiguous segments, and insufficient neighbors prevent derived values. Projected 2D measurements cannot establish 3D accuracy.
- Default pose checkpoint: Hugging Face usyd-community/vitpose-plus-small, configurable to a compatible checkpoint. A detector locates the single athlete before pose inference. Ball/racket tracking are future modules, not represented by fabricated results.
- Upload limits: 30 seconds, 100 MiB, bounded decoded dimensions. One tracking job at a time in this prototype, one API process. Temporary decoded files are cleaned after use.
- Local and production AI share prompts, tools, model (GPT-6.1 Sol), medium reasoning, validation, and review behavior. Only provider differs: local openai-oauth proxy when Codex is installed, OpenRouter otherwise; production uses OpenRouter. No silent paid-provider fallback.
- Coach receives actual sampled frame images and matching timestamped measurement text in bounded batches. Provider failure is visible; deterministic sample mode is explicitly labeled.
- Daytona provides the agent's file/process/browser environment even with the control plane on localhost. Only valid, screenshot-inspected configurations can be saved. Sandboxes auto-stop and are deleted after build completion; credentials stay on the control plane.
- Storage uses private buckets and short-lived signed reads. Database stores object keys, not permanent public URLs or video bytes.
- Locally run Vite, FastAPI, agent service, and OAuth proxy. Railway deployment is documented but not performed.

## Testing Decisions
The user subsequently requested pytest and Vitest suites, overriding the original brief’s no-new-suite preference. Add meaningful tests for height onboarding/edit, tab draft persistence, custom numeric entry and thumbnail load/error states, numerical contracts, provider parity, reviewed-draft acceptance, asynchronous UI state, video validation/derivatives, and atomic session updates. Verify known motion/angle/unit/error fixtures, typecheck/build, and backend lifecycle/failure boundaries. Prioritize actual laptop journeys: select a joint, open and close its custom details panel, verify plain Speed/Acceleration number and unit formatting, and confirm joint readings remain in the panel while KaTeX x=0/y=0 labels appear on white chips at their coordinate axes. Check vector direction, units, missing samples, fixed scales, edge fitting, and the ground estimate’s earliest-clear/bottom-clipped cases. Record actual CSS viewport and zoom separately from screenshot raster dimensions. Existing mobile/Daytona captures remain supplemental responsive evidence rather than a new mobile-polish requirement. Include a fresh live AI+Daytona reviewed build and real supplied-video inference. Keep synthetic, deterministic, local-live, and production evidence distinct. Capture representative laptop screenshots and an acceptance PDF; disclose remaining gaps.

## Out of Scope
Multiple-athlete management UI, authentication, payments, public deployment, live camera recording, multi-athlete clips, moving-camera compensation, 3D biomechanics, ball/racket detection in this phase, generated UI, post-build tool editing, historical compatibility, Jev, and claims of clinical measurement accuracy.

Dark mode is deferred to [GitHub issue #3](https://github.com/isaacwu2000/Milton-Blank-Page-Hacks/issues/3); this prototype implements no theme switch or dark palette.
